# eval_judge: measuring ">50 % as good as internet + frontier AI" (judges, bias, held-out construction)

State as of 2026-10-12. Builds on `websweep-2026-10-06/eval-methodology.md` (40 items: MT-Bench, AlpacaEval LC, RACE, CRAG, HealthBench, AA-Omniscience, SAFE/VeriScore, PoLL, PPI, Miller power formula, Leaderboard Illusion, Vicuna) and WEB-SWEEP-2026-10-09 §4 (2610.02877, 2511.21140, LongJudgeBench). Those items are not repeated except where new numbers were extracted. Nothing under blind/ or rnd/veil/ was read. 0 judge calls, 0 mini hours. Sources were opened as abstract pages, HTML full text (via fetch summaries), HF Hub API (licences) and GitHub API (licences, competitor notes). "fetch-summary" means a small model summarised the page; numbers marked so should be spot-checked before quoting in the claim.

## TL;DR

- **Our reference and judge are the same family, and the 2025–26 literature now names that effect.** "Preference leakage" (ICLR 2026) shows judges favour outputs related to them by the same model, inheritance or family. Rubric-based self-preference persists even on objective criteria: judges are ">50 % more likely" to wrongly pass their own failed rubric items, and on HealthBench rubrics the bias moves scores by up to 10 points. A 2026 family-panel study measures a same-family lift of +0.067 (CI 0.053–0.084), and one same-family judge flips 18.5 % of pairwise outcomes. → v0.3 (non-Claude writer and references) fixes only the item side. A non-Claude judge is still missing. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01534 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06996 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17857
- **GDPval gives a defensible "as good as" headline: win-or-tie rate against the reference.** Blind pairwise graders mark each output better / as good as / worse. The best model reached 47.6 % wins+ties against human experts. Human–human agreement was 71 % and the auto-grader's 66 %. Our 0/51 win-rate counts only wins. A wins+ties rate, with ties defined by the grader ("as good as"), is the published analogue of the bounty's wording. https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04374v1
- **Rubric scoring with signed weights is the 2025–26 standard for long-form research answers.** ResearchRubrics uses 101 prompts and 2,593 human-written criteria weighted −5…+5, scored as Σ w·verdict / Σ positive w. Judge–human macro-F1 is 0.72–0.76 on binary verdicts but falls to 0.53–0.57 on ternary ones. Grade binary, not 1–5. https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07685v1
- **Reference-guided judging is the single biggest judge-accuracy lever on long outputs.** LongJudgeBench (1,944 long outputs): reference raises mean judge accuracy from 0.533 to 0.587, and on the Verify subset from 0.549 to 0.817. Adding a rubric on top is not additive. The open-weight DeepSeek-V4-Flash with a reference (0.665) is close to the best proprietary judge (Qwen3-Max 0.674). https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01629
- **Small-n statistics now has a tool and hard rules.** evalstats (MIT, 2026-09) advises against bootstrap CIs at N < 100: in its simulations they were overconfident, and paired-binary bootstrap missed nominal coverage even at N = 100. It recommends PPI-corrected tests using about 25 human labels, ρ² ≥ 0.4 between judge and human before a judge is worth using, and Bonett-Price CIs for paired binary data. Shared-calibration bias correction can **reverse the sign** of a model difference (2605.06939). https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35815 · https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06939v1
- **Open judges: none is validated against Claude-level graders on long-form research QA.** Selene-1-Mini 8B (Apache-2.0) beats GPT-4o-mini on absolute scoring (0.648 vs 0.640 Pearson). Prometheus 2 (Apache-2.0) reaches Pearson 0.6–0.7 vs GPT-4 and 72–85 % agreement with humans. RocketEval reaches correlation 0.965 using Gemma-2-2B with checklists. In "Reliability without Validity" (21 judges), Kimi K2.5 and GPT-oss-120B reach JudgeBench κ 0.72 / 0.69 vs Opus 4.6 at 0.875. Our own JUDGE2 (Gemma 26B, κ 0.32/0.55) fits that picture: an open judge is good for ranking, not for the headline number. https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19544
- **Competitors' [competitor score removed] numbers are reproducible in principle.** Both public repos (a competitor team, a competitor team, MIT) publish their questions (BOAR v2's 61, MIT), answers, blind pairs, keys and scripts. Both use Opus 5.5 + web as the reference, 0–10 scoring by Claude graders who check facts on the web, and a share-of-reference ratio with no floor adjustment. Grader instances differ by 10–20 points. a competitor team's 40-item held-out set has not been run. A same-sitting regrade of all three apps' published answers by one grader is the only cross-app comparison that would mean anything. [competitor link removed] · [competitor link removed]
- **n for v0.3:** with paired per-item SD ≈ 0.094 (back-solved from MDE 0.025 at n = 110), n = 110 suffices for paired arm deltas of 0.025. The **absolute** claim (floor-adjusted vs 0.50) is a different estimand. Its CI half-width at n = 110 is about ±0.05 (V-para CI 0.39–0.49 at n = 51 implies a per-item SD of about 0.18). A ±0.03 half-width needs about 140 items (n = 51 would need about 280). These are my calculations, below.

**Mapping of our metrics.** Floor-adjusted ratio ≈ a mean rubric score rescaled to remove the decline floor, the same direction as CRAG, HealthBench and AA-Omniscience. Win-rate 0/51 = the strict "wins" half of GDPval's metric. No published design uses a ratio of means on a 1–5 Likert scale as a parity claim; the 10-06 sweep established this. New in this sweep: GDPval's wins+ties is the closest literal reading of "as good as", and ResearchRubrics' Σw·v/Σw⁺ is the closest reading of "x % as good" on long-form research answers (it is a percentage of the achievable criteria, with negative criteria).

**Minimum n (my calculation; check before quoting).** Paired arm delta: MDE ≈ 2.8·s_d/√n. Back-solving our MDE of 0.025 at n = 110 (1 seed) gives s_d ≈ 0.094. For MDE 0.03 that needs n ≈ (2.8·0.094/0.03)² ≈ 77, and arms that change many answers (s_d ≈ 0.2) need n ≈ 350. Absolute claim against the 0.50 bar: V-para CI 0.39–0.49 at n = 51 gives SE ≈ 0.026, so a per-item SD of about 0.18 (this includes seed variance). A ±0.03 half-width (95 %) needs n ≈ (1.96·0.18/0.03)² ≈ 140; n = 110 gives about ±0.034. Seed variance (SD ≈ 0.02–0.025 per run mean) adds about 0.02 to each single-seed run. Two seeds on 110 items keep the claim CI near ±0.035–0.04. **To call ">0.50" at 95 % one-sided, the point estimate must sit at ≈ 0.53–0.54 or above on v0.3 n = 110.**
