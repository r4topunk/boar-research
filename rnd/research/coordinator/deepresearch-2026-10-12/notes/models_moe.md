# models_moe: open-weight model ceiling under 12 GB RAM / ≤ ~35 GB disk with expert streaming (deep research 2026-10-12)

Angle: which open-weight MoE (≤ ~5B active, 20–150B+ total) could raise closed-book knowledge (LOSSMAP buckets *absent knowledge* and *wrong fact*) inside BOAR's constraints (Android, ≤ 12 GB RAM, ≤ 50 GB disk including the kit of ≈ 6.5 GB, offline, llama.cpp / llama.rn / bmoe, open licence). Builds on MODEL-LANDSCAPE-2026-10-06.md, websweep-2026-10-06/moe-on-device.md, WEB-SWEEP-2026-10-09/10-12, CLOSED.md (R17/R18 MoE screens, R37 QUANTCEIL) and SHELF.md (bmoe, llama.cpp #27794).
Method: HF Hub API (model cards, config.json, GGUF trees, summed per quant), GitHub API/pages (llama.cpp, BigMoeOnEdge, 19PINE-AI/ikp), arXiv abs/HTML, benchlm mirror of AA-Omniscience (updated 2026-10-10), WebSearch. ~45 tool calls, 40+ items examined. No downloads > 100 MB; nothing under blind/ or veil/ read. Sizes in GB = 10^9 bytes, summed over shards.

## TL;DR

1. **Primary per-tier knowledge data exist for open MoE models: the IKP repo (`19PINE-AI/ikp`, `data/results/evaluation_summary.json`, 201 models, pushed 2026-07-29).** Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B scores IKP 0.564 (T4 0.71, T5 0.22) vs Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-think 0.548 (T4 0.55, T5 0.21), Gemma 4 26B-A4B 0.452 (T4 0.35), Qwen3-30B-A3B-think 0.448 (T4 0.33). Qwen3.6-35B and Qwen3-4B are not in the file. [IKP results](https://github.com/19PINE-AI/ikp/blob/main/data/results/evaluation_summary.json)
2. **The only > 35B MoE that fits the 50 GB cap at ≤ 3B active: Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B** (Apache-2.0, 2025-09). UD-Q2_K_XL is 30.13 GB and UD-IQ2_XXS 26.21 GB. Upstream llama.cpp supports it (`qwen3next`, fixes merged through 2026-09). **BigMoeOnEdge does not list `qwen3next`**, but adding a standard `build_moe_ffn` arch is "one row" plus a gate. [GGUF](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct-GGUF) · [bmoe adding-a-model](https://github.com/Helldez/BigMoeOnEdge/blob/main/docs/adding-a-model.md)
3. **Knowledge vs size law (arXiv 2604.24827 v2, 2026-07-05):** IKP acc = 0.242 + 0.159·log10(N_B), R² 0.910 over 93 open models. For MoE, total params predict better (R² 0.67) than active params (0.41); total-param slope is 0.145 per decade. It predicts **+5.2 pp IKP for 35B → 80B and +8.0 pp for 35B → 125B**. For calibration, 4B → 35B is +13.7 pp, and BOAR measured that step as +0.06 knowledge. [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24827v2)
4. **Every other ≥ 60B low-active MoE is out.** Over the 50 GB cap: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next 125B-A6B (+51B n-gram table; ≥ 66.4 GB at any quant; licence "Qwen Community 1.0", not Apache), gpt-oss-120b (62.6 GB), Nemotron 3 Super 120B-A12B (≥ 52.7 GB), DeepSeek V4/V4.1 Flash (284B / 552B + 196B Engram). Too slow (≥ 6.5–12B active): Mistral Small 4 119B-A6.5B, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, GLM-4.5-Air, Sarvam-105B. No better knowledge than our 35B: Ling-3.0-flash 124B-A5.1B (AA 18.2 %). [HF trees summed](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GGUF) · [benchlm AA](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/omniscienceaccuracy)
5. **N-gram / engram ("the post author's" axis) has two open models, and neither is usable:**
   - Qwen3.8-Flash-Next carries a 51B n-gram table of 28.8 GB at IQ4_NL. bmoe leaves it mmap'd, with its device cost "not measured yet". Phone decode is 3.48 tok/s at Q2_K (80.4 GB) and 2.03 tok/s at UD-IQ3_XXS.
   - LongCat-Flash-Lite (68.5B, 31.4B of it n-gram embedding, ~3B active, MIT) runs only in an AI-generated llama.cpp fork. Upstream PRs #19167/#19182 are stalled, and its smallest GGUF is 30.5 GB (Q3_K_L).
   - LongCat's own base-model ablation shows **no MMLU gain from n-gram embedding** (64.01 vs 64.81).
   - [bmoe limitations](https://github.com/Helldez/BigMoeOnEdge/blob/main/docs/limitations.md) · [bmoe PR #189](https://github.com/Helldez/BigMoeOnEdge/pull/189) · [arXiv 2601.21204](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21204v2)
6. **Memory-layer families (Meta Memory Layers, UltraMem/V2, PEER, MoLE, FwPKM) have no open production-scale checkpoints.** The DeepSeek Engram llama.cpp PR #19654 was closed unmerged. Only research-scale checkpoints exist (MoLE: 160M active on 100B Pile tokens). [MoLE repo](https://github.com/JieShibo/MoLE) · [llama.cpp #19654](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/19654)
7. **Expert streaming of an 80B-A3B on a 12 GB phone has no direct measurement.**
   - BigMoMo (2609.14643) runs up to 80B on phones but gives speedups only (1.4–1.8× vs the best baseline), with no absolute tok/s for the 80B.
   - My arithmetic from config.json: Qwen3-Next reads 1.51B routed params per token vs 1.01B for Qwen3.6-35B, and its routed bank is 77.3B vs 32.2B. The same 4–5 GB cache therefore covers about 2.4× less of the bank. **Expect roughly 2–3.5 tok/s, vs 4–6 for the 35B (inference, unmeasured).**
   - [BigMoMo](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14643)
8. **Quantization: there is no MoE SimpleQA-style measurement at 2–3 bits.** For dense Qwen3.6 27B, KLD↔IKP r = −0.981 and "the most obscure are lost first" (Quesma, 2026-08-03). BOAR's own QUANTCEIL closed 2→3-bit on the 35B (+0.004). An 80B at 2-bit is therefore the untested combination: more stored facts, but the long tail is the first thing 2-bit erases. [Quesma](https://quesma.com/blog/quantization-hurts-knowledge)

## Q2. Knowledge vs total/active params; what it predicts for 80–125B at 3B active

Takeaway: three independent sources agree that stored knowledge tracks total parameters.
- **IKP law, primary:** a fitted slope.
- **Allen-Zhu:** a bits-per-parameter capacity bound.
- **Mixture of Parrots:** more experts at fixed active params keep improving memorization.

The law predicts only +5 pp IKP for 35B → 80B. The measured IKP per tier, however, shows Qwen3-Next-80B well ahead of Qwen3.5-35B on T4 (mid-tail) and level on T5–T6.

Prediction (my arithmetic from the IKP fit; it is an inference, not a measurement):
- With the total-param slope 0.145, 35B → 80B gives +5.2 pp and 35B → 125B gives +8.0 pp.
- BOAR's measured 4B → 35B step (Δ log10 = 0.94, i.e. +13.7 pp IKP) gave +0.06 "knowledge only". Scaling linearly, **35B → 80B ≈ +0.02 on BOAR**, below the V-para MDE of 0.03.
- The per-tier data argue for more: T4 +16 pp (200 probes per tier, SE ≈ 3.4 pp), against T5/T6 +1–2 pp.

Gaps (Q2): Qwen3.6-35B (the deep model) and Qwen3-4B are not in the IKP file, so the 80B-vs-ours gap is only bridged through Qwen3.5-35B (R17 found 3.5 ≈ 3.6 on BOAR). Qwen3-Next-80B was run in instruct mode and Qwen3.5-35B in thinking mode, which confounds the comparison. IKP results are BF16 API runs, not 2-bit.

## Q3. Quantization: knowledge retention at 2–3 bits

Takeaway: there is still no MoE factual-recall-vs-bits study. The best evidence is dense, plus KLD. For the 35B, 2→3-bit was not detected on BOAR (R37 QUANTCEIL). An 80B at ~2.7 bpw stores more raw bits (≈ 27 GB of experts vs ≈ 11 GB) than a 35B at any quant within its 12–22 GB, and that is the hypothesis the gate in Q2 tests.

## Q4. Expert-streaming feasibility for 60–130B total on a 12 GB phone with ≤ 50 GB disk

Takeaway:
- The only device numbers for > 35B are bmoe's, and all of them are over the disk cap: gpt-oss-120b at 2.2 tok/s, Flash-Next at 3.48 / 2.03 tok/s, and DeepSeek V4 Flash at 0.94 tok/s.
- An 80B-A3B is unmeasured. From config.json it reads about 1.5× more expert bytes per token than the 35B and has a 2.4× larger bank, so expect roughly half the 35B's decode speed.
- At that speed a thinking-512 answer would take about 2× the competitor's 3.5–5 min, so it is a no-thinking deep tier at best.

## Q5. n-gram / engram / memory-layer models (the post author's "most params on disk" axis)

Takeaway: the idea is now in production models (Qwen3.8-Flash-Next 51B table; DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 196B Engram; LongCat-Flash-Lite 31.4B). Lookup I/O is tiny: about 16 rows per token, KB-scale. None fits 50 GB with a usable engine:
- Flash-Next is ≥ 66 GB.
- DeepSeek V4.1 Flash is ≫ 100 GB.
- LongCat-Lite has no upstream llama.cpp support and showed no MMLU gain from the table.

No open product-key or memory-layer checkpoint exists above research scale.
