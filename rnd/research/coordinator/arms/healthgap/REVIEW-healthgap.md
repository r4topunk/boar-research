## MUST-FIX
1. Dose line, replace with (EN): "I don't have a reliable offline source on this, so I won't give medication doses from memory. Use the dose and the time between doses printed on the package, or ask a pharmacist or doctor. For a child, use the dose for their age or weight on the label and the measuring device that comes with the medicine; if the label doesn't list their age or weight, ask a pharmacist or doctor first. If someone may have taken more than the label's maximum, or the same ingredient from two different products, call a poison control center or your local emergency number now (911 in the US, 112 in Europe), even if they seem fine." (PT keeps 192 SAMU / 193 Bombeiros like `noHealthSourceAnswer`; ES like its ES line.) Reasons: paracetamol overdose has no early symptoms (NHS: get care even if you feel fine); FDA/CDC: use the device in the box, not a kitchen spoon; duplicate-ingredient products are a common accidental overdose.
2. `poisoning` card, replace with (EN): "Possible poisoning (swallowed or breathed in a chemical, a plant, a mushroom, alcohol or too much medicine): if they're unconscious, having a seizure, struggling to breathe, or very drowsy or confused, call emergency services now. If they're unconscious but breathing, lay them on their side; if they're not breathing normally, start CPR. Otherwise call a poison control center or your local emergency number now, even if they seem fine. If they breathed in fumes, get them into fresh air. Don't make them vomit, and don't give anything to eat or drink unless a poison center or doctor tells you to. Note what they took, how much and when, and keep the container, packet or a piece of the plant or mushroom to show." (~690 chars; longest existing EN card `headinjury` 1282 → OK.)
3. Card precedence (rule §2): a specific card from `regexCondition` (battery, co, eyechem, snakebite, stings…) wins over `poisoning`; `poisoning` is the fallback. A swallowed button battery must get the battery card ("go to the emergency department now"), never "call poison control". Keep battery/coin/magnet terms in the predicate (they force the health route), not in the card's choice.
4. Class precedence when several classes fire (seen: "Dad took 10 of his heart pills by mistake", "Took 2 Tylenol PM and a beer, too much?" fire DOSE+POISON): poison-event > co > dose > poison-info > survival.
5. Predicate FP/miss fixes, the lists below (all of them or the rule stays as it is). CO exclusion bug: `CO_EXCLUDE` is unbounded substrings and also vetoes "carbon monoxide" ("carbon monoxide alarm by the pool heater" → no fire; "lead" matches "misleading"). Fix: "carbon monoxide"/"monóxido de carbono" never excluded; exclusion word-bounded, without `pool`.
6. Multi-turn (§6): extending `isHealthFollowUp`'s words does not close the Calpol split: its gate is `isHealthQuestion(last)`, whose lexicon has no drug names, and "more"/"have"/"when can" are in neither FOLLOW_UP_WORDS nor FOLLOW_INTENT, so it is false whatever words are added. Use this instead: if the current turn is ≤ 12 words and every word ∈ FOLLOW_UP_WORDS ∪ {more, have, another, again, dose, doses, much, many, too, safe, next, one}, evaluate `healthGapClass(`${last}. ${query}`)`. Checked: "I gave her Calpol at 9." + "when can she have more?" → dose. The word guard is required: the plain concatenation also fires on "…when can I visit Lisbon again?". Ship it in v1 (n03 is a known probe), with a golden test for each of the two cases.
