# First-aid cards round 3: raw source verification (fetched 2026-10-03)

How these were fetched: same method as rounds 1–2. Every page was downloaded raw with curl and turned into text locally with the same HTML → text converter. CDC pages use a plain `curl/8.0` User-Agent; all other pages use the browser User-Agent. Pregnancy, Birth and Baby (healthdirect's child-health site) ships its article text inside a JSON blob, so that snapshot was converted by stripping tags and unescaping the JSON text (`\n`, `\"`, HTML entities). Every row below is **curl**; no row needed a summarising fetch, so there are no † rows. The one summarising fetch attempted (the Wilderness Medical Society guideline on journals.sagepub.com) returned HTTP 403 and produced nothing (see Not found). No personal data was sent in any request.

Licence key: **OGL** = UK Open Government Licence v3.0 (nhs.uk). Adaptations must not be presented as NHS content. **PD** = US federal public domain (CDC, Ready.gov). **©** = copyright (SJA, British Red Cross, American Red Cross, healthdirect / Pregnancy Birth and Baby, WHO, TravelHealthPro, Earthquake Country Alliance), so reuse facts only and do not copy wording into cards.

Convention: text in "double quotes" is verbatim from the page; text in ‘single quotes’ is draft-claim wording or a paraphrase, never a source quote. Quotes separated by " / " are separate list items or sentences on the same page.

## Conflicts

1. **BURN-COOL water: cool vs cold, 20 min vs ‘at least’.** NHS, SJA, healthdirect and PBB say "cool" running water; BRC says "cold". NHS gives exactly 20 minutes and is the only UK source with the "within 3 hours" window (healthdirect/PBB: "up to 3 hours"). SJA, BRC and healthdirect say "at least 20 minutes"; PBB says "around 20 minutes". No source says ‘lukewarm’.
2. **BURN-COVER on the face.** healthdirect: [quote withheld] NHS, SJA and BRC give no face exception.
3. **BURN-CALL criteria differ by source.** NHS (999/A&E): very large or deep; face, genitals or bottom; acid, chemical or electrical. NHS does not list hands, feet, joints, white or charred skin, smoke, breathing, or children. healthdirect adds hands, brown/black/white patches, and throat/airway. Feet, major joints and smoke inhalation come only from PBB (a child page). BRC: [quote withheld], plus "immediate medical advice" for the face or genitals. A card that lists all of the draft's criteria has to cite healthdirect + PBB, not NHS alone.
4. **BURN-CHILD tier.** BRC ([quote withheld]) and PBB ([quote withheld]) send every child to care. NHS sends a child to 111 only if you are [NHS quote withheld], or for advice for a child under 5.
5. **FROST-REWARM temperature.** No fetched source gives 37–39 °C. SJA: "around 40°C". healthdirect: "around 40 to 42°C". NHS and CDC give no figure ("warm (not hot)"; CDC: "comfortable to the touch for unaffected parts of the body"). The 37–39 °C figure belongs to the WMS guideline, which could not be fetched (paywall/403). A card should say "warm, not hot" (NHS/CDC) or cite one figure with its source.
6. **HEAT-CALL sweating.** NHS: [NHS quote withheld]. CDC/NIOSH lists "Hot, dry skin or profuse sweating", and healthdirect says sweating may persist after exercise. A card that says ‘not sweating’ as a required sign would under-call exertional heatstroke.
7. **HEAT-COOL cold-pack sites.** NHS and SJA: armpits and neck. CDC/NIOSH: head, neck, armpits and groin (and an ice bath if possible). healthdirect: "cheeks, palms and soles". BRC: pour cold water over a wet sheet until 38 °C.
8. **HEAT-EXH ‘lie down with feet raised’ (contested in round 1).** The current NHS page (reviewed 28 May 2026) has **no** lie-down or raised-feet step. Its 4 steps are: cool place, remove clothing, fluids, cool the skin. Only SJA says [quote withheld] (legs, not feet). BRC: "get them to rest". healthdirect: "Find a cool place to rest". The raised-legs step rests on SJA alone.
9. **NOSE-CALL time threshold and blood thinners.** NHS: A&E if the bleed lasts [NHS quote withheld]. SJA: call 999 if it lasts "more than 30 minutes" (after three 10-minute pinches). BRC child page: seek medical advice after [quote withheld]. NHS puts blood thinners (warfarin) in the **GP** tier, not A&E.
10. **BITE-CARE / BITE-CALL tiers.** healthdirect: any bite that breaks the skin → "get medical advice immediately". NHS gives criteria for 111 only (hands, feet, face or head; human bite; tetanus not up to date; infection signs) and A&E only for large/deep wounds or bleeding that cannot be stopped. There is no 999-specific bite tier in any source. A face bite is 111 in NHS but the emergency department in healthdirect.
11. **BITE-WASH duration and water.** WHO and CDC: soap and water for 15 minutes. TravelHealthPro: running tap "for several minutes", then wash. NHS: [NHS quote withheld], no duration. healthdirect: 15 minutes only for bat bites. NHS also tells you to squeeze a non-bleeding wound so it bleeds slightly (healthdirect agrees); no other source mentions this.
12. **QUAKE-BED posture.** Ready.gov: "turn face down" and cover head and neck. CDC: "hold on and stay there", head under a pillow. ARC: stay and cover head and neck. The sources do not contradict each other, but ‘face down’ comes only from Ready.gov.
13. **FROST-REFREEZE wording.** healthdirect: [quote withheld] (refreezing causes more damage). The draft's ‘don't thaw if it could refreeze before you reach care’ is not in that sentence.
