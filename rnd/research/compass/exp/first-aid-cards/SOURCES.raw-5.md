# First-aid cards round 5: raw source verification (fetched 2026-10-03)

How these were fetched: same method as rounds 1–4. Every page was downloaded raw with curl and turned into text locally with an HTML → text converter (`h2t.py`; PDFs with `pdftotext`, no layout). CDC, NWS and MedlinePlus pages use a plain `curl/8.0` User-Agent; all other pages use the browser User-Agent. Every row below is **curl**; there are **no † rows**. Summarising fetches (WebFetch) were tried only for pages curl could not get (Mayo Clinic, Paraná fire department); both returned HTTP 403 and produced nothing (see Not found). Web searches were used only to find URLs. No personal data was sent in any request.

Licence key: **PD** = US federal public domain (NOAA Ocean Today, NWS, NOAA NOS, CDC/NIOSH). **©** = copyright (MedlinePlus encyclopedia text is A.D.A.M. content; Brazil Ministério da Saúde / SES-SP; Mexico Secretaría de Salud / STCONAPRA, Morelos, Guanajuato), so reuse facts only and do not copy wording into cards.

Convention: text in "double quotes" is verbatim from the page; text in ‘single quotes’ is draft-claim wording or a paraphrase, never a source quote. Quotes separated by " / " are separate list items or sentences on the same page.

## Conflicts

1. **RIP order: float first vs swim out first.** NOAA Ocean Today: stay calm, "Instead - float!", then swim parallel only "If you're a good swimmer". NWS "Know Your Options": relax → don't swim against → swim out of the current → if you can't escape, float or tread water. NWS Brownsville: swim parallel; if you can't escape, float or tread water. The card says calm + float first, then swim parallel 'if you can swim'.
2. **RIP after escaping: angle vs waves.** NWS (social media, Brownsville) and NOAA NOS: back to shore "at an angle". NOAA Ocean Today: "Swim with the waves, allowing them to push you to shore." The card says 'at an angle back toward the beach'.
3. **RIP bystander: 'never enter the water' vs 'only with a flotation device'.** NOAA Ocean Today: "don't try to be a hero and make the rescue yourself". NWS: "Never enter the water without a floatation device" (so entering with one is allowed); NWS summer page: "use a floatation device if you go in the water". The card says 'Don't swim out to rescue them yourself' (the stricter reading).
4. **SCORPION cold vs warm (same as round 1).** CDC/NIOSH: "Apply ice, but do not submerge the affected area in ice water." MedlinePlus: ice in a cloth, 10 min on / 10 min off. Brazil SES-SP: "Aplicar compressa morna no local" and "[quote withheld]." A web-search summary attributes 'no ice' to Mexico too (Michoacán), but that page could not be fetched (Not found), and CONAPRA's ‘No apliques frío local’ is in its snakebite box. The default card has neither; the ice line is an ALTERNATIVE for r4to.
5. **SCORPION tier: every sting to a health service vs most need no treatment.** Brazil (SES-SP: "Para os demais pacientes: Deve-se procurar o mais rapidamente possível o serviço de saúde mais próximo"; MS FAQ: "encaminhada, sem demora") and Mexico (Morelos: "acudir de inmediato … dentro de los primeros 30 minutos"; Guanajuato: "acude de inmediato a tu centro de salud") vs MedlinePlus (US, A.D.A.M.): [quote withheld; the page says most North American stings need no medical treatment]. CDC/NIOSH: contact a health care provider or poison control. The card follows Brazil/Mexico.
6. **SCORPION higher-risk groups.** Children: all sources. Older adults: Guanajuato, Morelos, CONAPRA ("personas adultas mayores"). People with other illnesses: Morelos ("con comorbilidades"). MedlinePlus: children ≤ 6. No source names pregnancy.
