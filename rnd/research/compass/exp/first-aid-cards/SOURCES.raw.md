## Conflicts

1. **SCORP-3, cold vs warm.** CDC NIOSH says "Apply ice, but do not submerge…" (†). Brazil (SES-SP alert + federal PCDT 2025) says warm compress, and SES-SP explicitly says "Não colocar gelo ou água fria… pois acentua a dor." A card for Brazil should follow the Brazilian guidance or leave temperature out.
2. **BATT-HONEY.** poison.org recommends 10 mL every 10 min, up to 6 doses, age ≥12 months, within 12 h, en route to the ER. CAPT (UK) says UK doctors prioritise getting to A&E unless the trip is long. NHS WY says "Don’t let them eat or drink". So the UK omits honey and does not recommend it routinely.
3. **CHOKE-INFANT-2, chest-thrust technique.** NHS and BRC say two fingers, ‘middle of their chest just below the nipple(s)’. SJA says the "two-thumb encircling technique". None use the draft's ‘lower half of the breastbone’ wording.
4. **CHOKE-CHILD order.** NHS: "It doesn’t matter which order you do them in." BRC and SJA give a fixed sequence (back blows, then thrusts).
5. **DROWN-2.** SJA and BRC give 5 initial rescue breaths. Saudi MOH gives standard "2 rescue breaths". The timing of the call when the rescuer is alone differs: BRC says after 1 min of CPR, Saudi MOH after 2 min, SJA says use a speakerphone or helper and call immediately.
6. **DROWN-3, strength.** Saudi MOH says "must be taken to the hospital… even if they appear normal". AAP says this for anyone who needed any resuscitation. SJA says "may need to be observed… Medical advice should be sought, even if they appear well". BRC is silent. "Always hospital" is supported only by Saudi MOH.
7. **COIN-1 triage.** NHS WY Healthier Together sends every suspected coin swallow to A&E first. The draft's "no symptoms → see a doctor" tier is not supported by the NHS page. poison.org says management of asymptomatic cases is "controversial".
8. **HYPO-GLUCAGON / emergency call.** NHS calls 999 for an unconscious person only if glucagon is unavailable or fails, or alcohol is involved. St John NZ calls an ambulance for any unresponsive person. The draft's "call emergency" is fine, but NHS is conditional.
9. **CO-OUT.** NHS says "go outside" and does not mention opening windows. HSE says "Open all doors and windows" (for an appliance spilling CO). These do not contradict each other, but opening windows comes from HSE, not NHS.
10. **JELLY-CALL / CHILD-STING-MOUTH tiers.** On the NHS pages, mouth/throat/eye stings are a lower tier than 999 (minor injuries unit for jellyfish, urgent GP/111 for insects).
