## Conflicts

1. **HEART-ASPIRIN (chew / age).** Only SJA says both "chew it slowly" and "under 16". BRC says chew but gives no age limit. NHS (reviewed Mar 2026) and BHF (updated Mar 2026) give "300mg" plus the allergy caveat only, with no chewing and no age. A card that says ‘chew’ and ‘not under 16’ rests on SJA alone (plus BRC for chewing).
2. **HEAD-AE (tier drift).** NHS 111 Wales (Nov 2024, adapted from an older nhs.uk text) keeps a [NHS quote withheld] tier: vomiting, a headache not relieved by painkillers, behaviour change, memory problems, alcohol/drugs, blood thinners. The current nhs.uk page (reviewed 29 May 2025) has no A&E tier. It puts vomiting, dizziness, blood thinners and alcohol/drugs under **NHS 111**, moves behaviour change to **999**, and drops [NHS quote withheld] and ‘memory problems’. healthdirect makes vomiting more than once an **emergency**, and CDC sends "Vomit repeatedly" and a worsening headache to 911/ED. BRC calls 999 for any vomiting. The tiers disagree, so a card should pick one source and cite it.
3. **HEAD-HOME durations.** NHS: contact sport 3 weeks, alcohol [NHS quote withheld]. healthdirect: any sport and alcohol at least 48 h, and paracetamol only ("not other pain-relief medicines"); NHS England also names paracetamol, while NHS Wales says [NHS quote withheld].
4. **HEAD-NECK / HEAD-RECOVERY.** SJA: do not move a suspected spinal casualty unless in danger; support the head; jaw thrust; use the recovery position only if the airway cannot be maintained. BRC: [quote withheld] even with a suspected neck injury. Both say to keep the spine in line.
5. **TSU-VERTICAL threshold.** SENAPRED (Chile): only as a second option, buildings of "al menos ocho pisos". NSW SES: "upper floors of a sturdy building" (no height). NWS: ask local emergency management about vertical evacuation; "may be able to provide protection". Ready.gov is silent.
6. **TSU-QUAKE trigger.** Ready.gov: after the quake, move "if there are natural signs or official warnings" (the quake is itself listed as a natural sign). NWS: "strong or long earthquake" in a hazard zone, but any quake "no matter how big or how long" if on the beach. SENAPRED: a quake that makes it hard to stay standing. ARC: as soon as the shaking stops, near the coast. The draft's ‘strong or long’ matches NWS.
7. **HYPOTH-DONT vs CDC electric blanket.** CDC says to warm the core "using an electric blanket, if available" (and skin-to-skin). NHS forbids hot bath / hot water bottle / heat lamp, and SJA forbids direct heat near the casualty. These do not contradict each other directly, but a card that says ‘no external heat’ would contradict CDC.
8. **HYPOTH-CALL scope.** NHS: A&E or 999 for any suspected hypothermia. SJA and BRC: call 999 as a numbered step. CDC: get medical attention if the temperature is below 95 °F, and emergency help for severe or unconscious cases.
9. **HYPOTH-SKIN framing.** CDC presents skin-to-skin as a normal option, not ‘only if nothing else is available’. The draft's ‘if nothing else’ is not in any source.
