# First-aid cards round 4: raw source verification (fetched 2026-10-03)

How these were fetched: same method as rounds 1–3. Every page was downloaded raw with curl and turned into text locally with the same HTML → text converter (`h2t.py`). CDC pages use a plain `curl/8.0` User-Agent; all other pages use the browser User-Agent. Every row below is **curl**; there are no † rows. Two summarising fetches were attempted for pages curl could not get (American Red Cross "How to Apply a Tourniquet", NHS inform "Electric shock first aid"). Both returned HTTP 403 and produced nothing (see Not found). No personal data was sent in any request.

Licence key: **OGL** = UK Open Government Licence v3.0 (nhs.uk). Adaptations must not be presented as NHS content. **PD** = US federal public domain (CDC, CDC/NIOSH). **©** = copyright (SJA, British Red Cross, American Red Cross, Resuscitation Council UK, healthdirect, ASCIA), so reuse facts only and do not copy wording into cards.

Convention: text in "double quotes" is verbatim from the page; text in ‘single quotes’ is draft-claim wording or a paraphrase, never a source quote. Quotes separated by " / " are separate list items or sentences on the same page.

## Conflicts

1. **BLEED-SOAK: add on top vs remove and replace.** NHS (cuts page, reviewed Apr 2026): "apply another bandage or cloth on top". SJA: "remove it and reapply pressure with a new dressing or pad". BRC: "Remove the item and replace with a fresh one". The two UK first-aid charities agree with each other and contradict NHS. A card has to choose one and cite it, or say ‘keep firm pressure; if it soaks through, replace or add a pad and press harder’ with both sources shown.
2. **BLEED-RAISE.** Only NHS still says to raise the wound ("raise it above your head" / "above the level of your heart"). SJA's severe-bleeding steps and BRC's 3 steps have no wound-raising step. Both raise the **legs** only, for shock (SJA: "Raise and support their legs"; BRC: "lift their feet higher than the rest of their body"). RCUK 2025 has no raise step either.
3. **STROKE mnemonic and TIA tier.** UK (NHS, SJA, BRC, RCUK): FAST. CDC (EN): **BE FAST** (adds Balance, Eyes). CDC (ES): **RÁPIDO** (R/A/P/I/D/O), a different letter mapping. For symptoms that go away, NHS says call 999 (signs "within the last 24 hours even if they've now stopped"). CDC calls it a possible TIA and says to tell a health care team "right away", not 911. Only CDC says not to let someone else drive you; NHS says "Do not drive yourself".
4. **ANAPH-LIE legs.** NHS: "you can raise your legs" (optional). SJA: "lying the casualty down with their legs raised". RCUK: lying down, or sit "with their legs extended" if breathing is hard. healthdirect: lie flat; sit "with legs outstretched" if breathing is hard or vomiting. ‘Don't stand or walk’ is on NHS and healthdirect only (and healthdirect's treatment text). NHS's 999 wording is ‘anaphylactic reaction’; SJA's is ‘anaphylaxis’.
5. **EYECHEM contact lenses.** SJA: ask them to remove lenses if they can. healthdirect: try to take them out, **but not** if the eye surface is badly damaged. NHS gives no lens step at the time of injury (only "do not wear contact lenses until your eye is better"). Rinse time: NHS/SJA "at least 20 minutes"; healthdirect "20 minutes". NHS limits 999/A&E to a **strong** chemical (oven cleaner, bleach).
6. **ELEC-HV distance.** The draft's 18 m was not found in any fetched source. healthdirect: **6 metres** from a downed power line. American Red Cross: **35 feet** (~10.7 m) from fallen lines "and anything they are touching". CDC/NIOSH: 10 feet from energized parts (workplace rule for unqualified workers, not a downed-line rule). The SJA electrocution page covers **low voltage only** and gives no distance.
7. **ALT-STOP descent trigger.** NHS: descend if symptoms "get worse or do not improve after 1 day" (300–1,000 m lower). CDC Yellow Book: descend "if the symptoms become worse despite rest or treatment"; AMS "generally resolves within 12–48 hours" if you don't go higher. ‘No alcohol’ and ‘≤500 m higher per night’ are **prevention** advice in both, not advice for when you have symptoms. CDC's ‘never ascend to sleep higher with symptoms’ has no NHS equivalent (NHS: "do not travel or climb to a higher altitude" while resting).
8. **MARINE hot water: time, temperature and spines differ by creature.** NHS (all UK sea creatures): "as hot as can be tolerated" for **at least 30 minutes**, remove spines. healthdirect: fish/stingray **45 °C for up to 90 minutes**; stonefish "up to 30 minutes" and **leave the spine in** unless told otherwise; sea urchin 20 minutes, remove surface spines. Call tier: NHS sends a stingray sting to a **minor injuries unit** (999 only for breathing problems, chest pain, collapse and similar). healthdirect: **000** for stonefish; hospital for a deep fish/stingray wound or a lot of pain. No source names stingray wounds to the chest or abdomen.
9. **TICK removal: UK/US vs Australia (direct contradiction).** CDC, NHS and SJA: pull out with fine-tipped tweezers. SJA explicitly says "Do not burn or **freeze** the tick". healthdirect: kill it first by freezing with an ether spray, "Do not try to remove ticks with tweezers", and if it won't freeze, leave it in place and get urgent help. This applies to **everyone**, not only allergic people. ASCIA: freeze first; tweezers between skin and tick are "unsafe"; people with tick allergy should have it removed in an emergency department. A card has to be region-scoped (AU vs rest), or it will give dangerous advice in one region. Rash/fever watch window: CDC "several weeks"; NHS/SJA "3 months".
10. **HEXH-LIE.** Unchanged from round 3: NHS (reviewed 28 May 2026) has 4 steps and no lie-down or raised legs. SJA: "lie down in a cool, shaded place and raise their legs". 999 tier: NHS = not better after 30 minutes, or heatstroke signs. SJA = "if you are concerned".
11. **BLEED-TOURNIQUET wording.** RCUK: apply "as soon as possible" for life-threatening limb bleeding not controlled by direct pressure, 5–7 cm above the wound, write the time, don't release. SJA: catastrophic limb bleeding (amputation/blast), or when severe bleeding continues after pressure. SJA expects an **improvised** tourniquet only on 999 call-handler instruction. BRC: only inside PAcT kits ("severe, sustained and uncontrolled"). SJA and RCUK agree on ‘don't loosen’.
