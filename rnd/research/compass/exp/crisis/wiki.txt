A '''suicide crisis line''' (also known as a [[crisis hotline]]) is a service that provides immediate, confidential support to people experiencing emotional distress or [[suicidal ideation]] via telephone, text messaging, or online chat.<ref name="WHO2018">{{cite web |title=Preventing suicide: a resource for establishing a crisis line |url=https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO_MSD_MER_18.4 |publisher=World Health Organization |year=2018 |access-date=July 10, 2026}}</ref> They are connected with trained counselors who offer [[crisis intervention]], emotional support, and referrals to appropriate [[mental health]] resources.<ref name="SAMHSA988">{{cite web |title=988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline |url=https://www.samhsa.gov/find-help/988 |publisher=Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration |access-date=July 10, 2026}}</ref> Each hotline typically operates within a single nation or region and is run by a government agency, a non-profit organization, or a charity.<ref name="IASP">{{cite web |title=Crisis Centres & Helplines |url=https://www.iasp.info/crisis-centres-helplines/ |website=International Association for Suicide Prevention |access-date=July 10, 2026}}</ref>

While many countries have dedicated mental health crisis lines, a significant number of nations and territories—particularly in Sub-Saharan Africa and Central Asia—do not.<ref name="FindAHelpline">{{cite web |title=Find A Helpline |url=https://findahelpline.com/ |publisher=ThroughLine |access-date=July 10, 2026}}</ref> In regions where a general service is unavailable, specialized hotlines may operate for [[violence against women]]<ref name="LilaHelp">{{cite web |title=Find trustworthy gender-based violence helplines and support services |url=https://lila.help/ |website=Lila.help |publisher=Global Network of Women's Shelters |access-date=July 10, 2026}}</ref> or [[child welfare]].<ref name="ChildHelplineIntl">{{cite web |title=Global Data on Child Helplines |url=https://www.childhelplineinternational.org/ |website=Child Helpline International |access-date=July 10, 2026}}</ref> Alternatively, people in these countries can receive support through international organizations such as [[Befrienders Worldwide]],<ref name="Befrienders">{{cite web |title=Befrienders Worldwide |url=https://www.befrienders.org/ |publisher=Befrienders Worldwide |access-date=July 10, 2026}}</ref> [[Lifeline (crisis support service)#History|Lifeline International]],<ref name="LifelineIntl">{{cite web |title=LifeLine International |url=https://lifeline-international.com/ |publisher=LifeLine International |access-date=July 10, 2026}}</ref> and the mental health programs of the [[International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies]] (IFRC).<ref>{{cite web |title=Mental health and psychosocial support |url=https://www.ifrc.org/our-work/health-and-care/community-health/mental-health-and-psychosocial-support |website=International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies |access-date=July 10, 2026}}</ref>

Even when a general crisis line is available, people from marginalized communities—such as [[LGBT|LGBTQ+]] individuals<ref name="TransSolidarity">{{cite web |last=Benito |title=LGBTQIA+ Hotlines & Crisis Care: Free Global Support Directory |url=https://transsolidarityproject.org/lgbtqia-hotlines/ |website=Trans Solidarity Project |date=5 June 2026 |access-date=July 10, 2026}}</ref> or survivors of [[domestic violence]] or [[sexual abuse]]<ref name="NoMoreGlobalDirectory">{{cite web |title=NO MORE Global Directory |url=https://nomoredirectory.org/ |website=NO MORE |publisher=NO MORE Foundation |access-date=July 10, 2026}}</ref>—may prefer to utilize specialized hotlines tailored to their specific identities or circumstances.

The following list of general suicide crisis lines is organized by geographic region and arranged alphabetically by country within each region.
