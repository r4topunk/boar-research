A phone in airplane mode

Imagine you are on a mountain, on a long flight, or somewhere the network is down. You still have questions: what is this plant, how far is the next town, what does this error mean. BOAR keeps a small AI model and a few downloaded libraries on your phone and answers from them, with sources.

The bounty's rules

The challenge had strict limits: an Android phone with at most 12 GB of memory, at most 50 GB of downloaded files, fully offline, open source. And one target: "more than 50 % as good as internet search plus a frontier AI." Winning is decided by a public confirmation, not by a score.

Half as good as what, exactly?

That sentence hides a research problem. Half of what? Measured how, on which questions, on which phone? If you grade a student's essay against a professor's, "half as good" depends on who grades and what counts. Before improving anything, we had to build a way to measure that we could trust.

Where we started

When the author joined the project, the app ran a small 1.5-billion-parameter model over about 1,300 Wikipedia paragraphs. A review called it "solid engineering, weak research": good code, but nobody knew how good the answers were.

The problem as a spec

The spec: Android, ≤ 12 GB RAM, ≤ 50 GB, offline, half as good

The bounty fixes the hardware envelope and leaves the quality target vague. Hard limits: Android or GrapheneOS, at most 12 GB of RAM, at most 50 GB of assets, fully offline, no Play Services, public source. Target: "more than 50 % as good as internet search plus a frontier AI" on explanation, comparison, synthesis and reasoning questions, in English. We treated the envelope as a budget and the target as a measurement problem: before touching the engine we built a desktop runner that executes the app's real TypeScript answer engine, and a judge that compares every answer with a frozen frontier-plus-web reference.

Abstract

This is the record (v0.4-preprint, as of 2026-10-25; research lines closed 2026-10-23) of a five-week attempt to build an offline Android research assistant that is "more than 50 % as good as internet search plus a frontier AI", under a ≤ 12 GB RAM, ≤ 50 GB storage envelope (poidh bounty #31). Quality was first measured pairwise against frozen references written by Claude Opus with web search, judged by Claude Opus in both presentation orders on a 1–5 rubric, and reported as a raw ratio, a floor-adjusted ratio that removes the rubric minimum, and win/tie/loss; every deciding criterion was hashed before its run and held-out exposure was logged per touch. On a bounty-style set held out at run time (V-para, n = 51, two seeds) the frozen 4B build scores raw 0.413 (0.384–0.445), floor-adjusted 0.261 (0.224–0.300), and wins 0 of 51; a 35B mixture-of-experts with a 512-token thinking budget reaches floor-adjusted 0.436 (0.401–0.472) on development data, desktop only, 0 wins, and was not delivered. A cross-family judge raises all levels by ≈ 0.10 while preserving the ranking and a near-zero win-rate. Phone constraints then made a 1.7B model the main 8 GB engine (the 4B leads it by +0.109 under Opus on development data). A second ruler (75 items, half written by each judge family, frozen before any answer; U-total with a zero for non-answers; verdict = lower of two families) has been run on the desktop twin of the submitted app's pre-rename engine (one seed): 0 of 71 wins under both families and a lower-family U-total of 0.164 (0.123–0.206), below the 0.50 bar; we report it as history, because the headline is the same ruler run on the phone with the shipped app under a criterion frozen before generation, which landed on 2026-10-25. Our thesis is a design statement: small models need external knowledge, so we built a harness around a small model's real capabilities (health gate, code-computed arithmetic, pack search and delivery, a short answer style, a fixed check-the-source line, refusals). Its evidence and limits come from closing tests on pack-written self-tests: with the item's own article delivered, the 1.7B is right on 65 % of items vs 28 % otherwise (Claude Haiku outcome labels, one seed), delivery succeeds on 54 %, no pack reaches 0.75, and on real development items delivery does not separate answer quality. As of 2026-10-25, under the Claude judge, the 0.50 bar was not reached on the earlier held-out sets, and no unexposed held-out number exists. The submitted app, LabRat (formerly BoarLite), runs offline on an 8 GB phone; one search change in it was merged although its pre-registered safety criterion failed by the letter, under a trade-off its author accepted in advance, and we report both. We release the first benchmark and judge, a registry of negative results, openly licensed knowledge packs and a phone-parity study.

1 Introduction: an underspecified target and a measurable one

The target phrase "more than 50 % as good as internet plus frontier AI" leaves the construct, the comparator, the question distribution and the judge unspecified. We operationalise it as a ratio of judged rubric scores between the system's answer and a frozen frontier-plus-web reference for the same question, on sets drawn to resemble the bounty's stated use (explanation, comparison, synthesis, reasoning). We make four contributions: (i) a public benchmark and pairwise judge with frozen references and a fail-closed pipeline; (ii) a pre-registered evaluation of a production-shaped offline system across seven frozen builds and a 35B deep tier; (iii) an analysis of the measuring instrument itself (rubric floor, seed versus judge noise, judge-family offset, reference length); (iv) a registry of negative results with power statements. We also document an unusual organisation: the work was coordinated and executed by AI agents under one human author, with independent AI reviewers in place of human sign-off.
