One simple app: LabRat

Which app is submitted changed twice: the app maintainer's native app (decided 10-15, reaffirmed 10-21), then on 2026-10-24 BoarLite, a closed, offline, Succinct-only chat app of the author's own, renamed LabRat on 2026-10-25 ("LabRat (formerly BoarLite)"; below, BoarLite for events before the rename).

Full thesis

In one evening (2026-10-24 17:39 → 20:34) BoarLite went from a decision to a final candidate: a health gate that shows fixed safe text when no health source is kept, one fixed line under every answer, factbook v1, conversions computed in code, and the bench search ported in a mode that skips health questions. It runs offline on a Pixel 8a. The search port was merged although its frozen criterion failed by the letter, because the author had accepted that health trade-off in advance; the record says so. Its ruler scores exist on the desktop run of the earlier engine and are public as history (0 of 71 wins; lower-family U-total 0.164, below the 0.50 bar). That night the author cut the app down further (long-chat summary, PIN lock, Portuguese, the Expo/React Native tree), and on 2026-10-25 it became LabRat, whose assistant, Rat, is named by a new line in the grounding prompt: an engine change after the ruler run, so the ruler number belongs to the earlier engine and the final on-phone matrix (the final on-phone run, running) measures LabRat (the ruler run predates the Rat persona line).

Which app is the entry?

For most of the project the entry was going to be the app maintainer's own app. On 24 October the author decided instead on a simple app of his own, BoarLite. On 25 October, at the app maintainer's request, it got its own name: LabRat, whose assistant is called Rat.

One way to use it

One chat, one short answer style, sources shown, every library inside one download, and no permission to touch the internet. No deep mode, no long mode. Under every answer, one short line: "May contain errors; check the source."

A safe text instead of a guess

Ask the app how much of a medicine to give a child and it does not guess: unless it has found a trusted health source, it shows a fixed safe text with the emergency number. On 65 test questions written to probe exactly this, it shows that text on 41, including every question where the small model had made up a dose. The price: some health questions whose only source is a Wikipedia article also get the safe text instead of an answer. That limitation ships; fixing it is planned for after the submission. A better search, and the rule it broke

Porting the research engine's better search into the app got the right passage to the model more often (40 key facts instead of 26). But on the health test questions it also let a few more numbers into answers that no shown source backed up (6 instead of 4), even with the new search switched off for health questions. The rule written before the test said that is a fail. The author had said in advance he would accept a small health cost if the rest got better, and it did; so it shipped. We show both.

Less app, on purpose

In one night the author took things out: the long-chat summary (on the phone the small model only used the last three exchanges anyway), the PIN lock, Portuguese (the bounty is in English), and every screen and setting the closed app could not reach. The licences now ship inside the app.

A new name, and one new line

Renaming the app also changed one line of the instructions the model reads: it now introduces itself as Rat. That line came after the app's engine was graded on the new exam on a desktop copy (0 wins in 71, below the bar), so that grade belongs to the engine before the rename and is kept as history. The phone test measured LabRat as it ships, and its grade is the headline.

Where to get it

The download is too big for GitHub's file limit (3 GB), so it is hosted on Hugging Face with an install page. One open risk: Android's new developer checks may add a warning and a wait for apps installed from a browser; a test on an ordinary certified phone is still to be done.
