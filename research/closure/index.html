Research closed, a sentence tested

On 23 October the author closed the research and wrote down what he expected: "the app is right whenever the answer is in one of its libraries". Then we tested that sentence.

Right two times in three, when the page arrives

On each library's own questions, the small model was right 48 % of the time with the library installed and 26 % without. When search delivered the right article, it was right 65 % of the time, but search delivered it only about half the time. No library reached the 75 % mark we had fixed in advance. What we learned, and what we built

The tests support that first sentence only in a narrower form: right about two times in three when the right page reaches the model. On real questions, the libraries did not help in a measurable way. What the author takes from it, and states as the thesis (25 October 2026): small models need outside knowledge, so the app is a harness built around what a small model can really do: a safe text instead of a guess on health, sums done by code, a search that brings the right page, one short answer style, a line asking you to check the source, and a fixed refusal text where it should not guess. The numbers above are its evidence and its limits; they do not make the app half as good as the frontier. The author restated the thesis on 2026-10-25: small models need external knowledge; the project learned that and built a harness around a small model's real capabilities, tuned for the app user's experience (health gate, QuickMath, pack search and delivery, the Succinct persona, a fixed check-the-source line, refusal behaviour). Closing tests and the engine release candidate

Acceptance tests on a desktop twin, one closed engine, a judge pass the next morning

After the author closed the research lines (2026-10-23 11:53), the remaining work was acceptance tests on the app's desktop twin, scored with outcome labels (Claude Haiku 5.5-low; correct-any = right with the right source shown + right from memory; WU = wrong with no warning). The pack test (180 pack-written self-tests of 9 packs, 1.7B Succinct, seed 42): correct-any 0.48 with the pack vs 0.26 without, 0.65 when the item's own article is kept (54 % of items), 0 of 9 packs at 0.75. The answer-style test (60 dev items): Succinct beats Detailed with and without the pack; with the pack off every honest "unknown" is an engine decline. Pack shaping (factbook rewritten as short sentences that name the country) doubled fact delivery (7 → 15 of 20) and correct answers (5 → 10) on its own self-tests, but missed its frozen rule by one new warned confident error. Overnight one closed tree, the engine release candidate (the search fixes + shaped factbook + a reworded banner), passed every 0-LLM suite (identity 97/97 and 180/180, routes 103/110, the one letter-level failure pre-existing) and generated 2,855 answers with no judge call; the next day's judge kept Succinct (correct-any 0.43 vs 0.42 for longer styles, wrong-unwarned 0.12 vs 0.23–0.25).

Thesis box

"Small models need external knowledge. We learned that, and built a harness around a small model's real capabilities, tuned for the app user's experience."
