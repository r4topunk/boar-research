The research · Act IV, From the bar to a product · Chapter 13

Delivery, not coverage

Read atCuriousTechAI pros

With the 1.7B as main engine, the question became whether the one passage that holds the answer reaches the model. On questions written from the packs it often does, and answers jump; on real development questions the pack gain disappears, and almost every undelivered fact already sits in an installed pack. Search fixes delivered more facts; no answer-level gain was established, and one fix was withdrawn as unsafe.

How you ask decides what it finds

Ask a yes/no question and the right page is found 83 % of the time; ask a bare follow-up like "and how long does it take?" and it is found 8 % of the time. The book is on the shelf

Of 50 facts the app failed to bring to the model, 44 were already in a library on the phone. The problem was not missing knowledge; it was fetching it.

Better fetching, one fix withdrawn

Search fixes doubled how often the right passage arrived (21 → 42 of 92 facts). One more fix did better still, but it let unsafe health answers through on test prompts, so we withdrew it. Answers did not measurably improve.

Delivery: getting the fact-bearing chunk to a 1.7B

Count delivery at chunk level; most misses are search, not missing content

We count a fact as delivered only when its quote sits inside one kept chunk; article-level counting had overstated delivery by 8–13 facts. On 92 keyed dev items, delivered facts went from 21 (no fix) to 28 with a global-IDF merge (word rarity computed across all installed packs, so small packs compete fairly; +6.2 points hit@kept, 0 lost), to 42 with three Stack Exchange search fixes (the search fixes), and to 47 with a fourth fix (F3, a health no-source fallback) that fired on 21 of 65 safety probes and raised unsafe answers from 9 to 13, so it was withdrawn. Answer-level gains stayed within noise: the search fixes +0.013 (−0.008…+0.035) on all 97 (Haiku, two seeds). Of the 50 facts still undelivered, 44 already sit in the default 13-pack kit. A compact per-pack index (W78) cut the data read per question from 125.09 to 21.26 MB and the cold first stage from 620 to 92 ms on a desktop replay.
