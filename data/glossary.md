# RatLab glossary

- **BOAR** [G-boar]: An Android app that answers questions with no internet, using a small AI model and libraries of text stored on the phone. Technical: Offline research assistant: deterministic router + instant tier + hybrid retrieval (SQLite FTS5 + on-device embeddings) over knowledge packs + a local reader LLM (Qwen3-4B Q4_K_M), optional 35B MoE deep tier.
- **poidh bounty #31** [G-bounty]: A public crypto bounty asking for an offline Android research app that is "more than 50 % as good as internet plus a frontier AI", on a phone with at most 12 GB of RAM, in English. Technical: Open bounty on poidh; acceptance is a public confirmation by the issuer, not a numeric benchmark. Constraints: offline, ≤ 12 GB RAM, ≤ 50 GB storage, English.
- **the bar (0.50)** [G-bar]: The target: BOAR's answers should be worth at least half of what a frontier AI with web search gives. Technical: Operationalised as floor-adjusted ratio CI low ≥ 0.50 (primary target, from 2026-10-06) and raw ratio CI low > 0.50 on both V-para and v0.2 (formal rule of the 2026-10-02 analysis plan). As of 2026-10-18, not reached under the Claude judge on held-out data; the research is open and pending runs could change this (data/status.json).
- **frontier reference answer** [G-reference]: For every test question, a top AI model with web search wrote one answer; it is frozen and every BOAR answer is compared with it. Technical: Claude Opus with web search, prompt reference.v1, generated once per item, hashed (sha256), never regenerated, so every run shares the same denominator.
- **pairwise judge (both orders)** [G-pairwise-judge]: An AI judge reads the question and both answers side by side, scores both, then reads them again in the swapped order to cancel any bias for whichever comes first. Technical: judge.v1: Claude Opus scores both answers on three 1–5 axes and picks a winner, in A/B and B/A order; scores averaged over orders; order disagreement on the winner = tie. Links, source lists and citation markers are stripped (blinding).
- **rubric (correctness, completeness, usefulness)** [G-rubric]: Each answer gets three scores from 1 to 5: is it right, is it complete, is it useful. Technical: mean-rubric = mean of the three 1–5 axes, averaged over both presentation orders. The scale has a floor of 1, which inflates ratios (see rubric floor).
- **raw ratio** [G-raw-ratio]: BOAR's total score divided by the reference's total score. It flatters BOAR, because even a useless answer gets 1 out of 5. Technical: Σ mean-rubric(BOAR) / Σ mean-rubric(reference) over judged items; the pre-registered formal metric (analysis plan, 2026-10-02).
- **rubric floor / always-decline baseline** [G-rubric-floor]: Because the lowest score is 1, not 0, an app that refuses every question already earns about 0.31 on the raw ratio. Technical: Always-decline baseline D = 0.306 raw (79 judged declines over 7 runs; v0.2-weighted 0.307). About 61 % of a raw 0.50 is therefore "free".
- **floor-adjusted ratio** [G-floor-adjusted]: The same ratio after removing the free point from both sides: how much of the reference's value above "nothing" BOAR delivers. This is the headline number. Technical: Σ(S − 1) / Σ(R − 1) with S, R the mean-rubric scores of BOAR and the reference. Pre-registered as a companion metric (2026-10-02), headline since the 2026-10-06 audit.
- **win / tie / loss and win-rate** [G-win-rate]: For each question: did the judge prefer BOAR's answer, the reference, or neither? BOAR won 0 of 51 bounty-style questions. Technical: Per-item combined winner over both orders (disagreement = tie). Reported, never gated (since 2026-10-10). 0 wins on every Claude-judged row of V-para and v0.2; isolated wins on v0.1 dev only.
- **formal raw rule vs primary target** [G-formal-rule]: There are two ways to read the bar; the stricter one (floor-adjusted) is the one the project leads with, and as of 2026-10-18 it has not been met. Technical: Formal rule (plan 2026-10-02): raw CI low > 0.50 on both sets — met only by the deep tier with thinking on dev data. Primary target (2026-10-06): floor-adjusted CI low ≥ 0.50 on V-para — met by no configuration as of 2026-10-18 (best 0.436, CI high 0.472).
- **L1 / L2 judges (triage and confirmation)** [G-l1-l2]: A cheap AI judge screens ideas first; the expensive judge only confirms promising ones and produces the published numbers. Technical: L1 = Claude Haiku 5.5-low (replaced Sonnet-low on 2026-10-07), N = 4 shards, both orders, on 20-item slices, paired vs a named control; never an absolute L1 ratio. L2 = Claude Opus judge.v1, both orders, two seeds, for confirmations and claim numbers. Haiku compresses gaps (0.70–0.95× Opus on pack arms).
- **near-miss rule** [G-near-miss]: If the cheap judge lands very close to a decision line, the expensive judge decides instead. Technical: A Haiku Δ within 0.01 of a criterion threshold is re-judged with Opus on the same ids before any verdict.
- **cross-family judge (XFAM)** [G-xfam]: The same answers were re-scored by an AI judge from a different company, to see whether a Claude judge treats Claude-written references kindly or harshly. Technical: Post-hoc re-judge of existing traces with OpenAI gpt-5.6-sol (via the Codex CLI), same rubric, orders and blinding (408 calls V-para, 660 v0.2). Scores ≈ +0.10 higher on both sets, ranking unchanged (Spearman 0.88–0.97), win-rate 0.00–0.06. Not pre-registered.
- **lower-judge rule** [G-lower-judge]: When two judges disagree, the project publishes the lower (less flattering) number as the headline. Technical: Claude Opus stays the headline judge because it is the stricter family; cross-family numbers are printed beside it, labelled post-hoc, and the judge family is named next to every ratio.
- **judge family / self-preference** [G-judge-family]: The questions, the reference answers and the judge all came from the same AI company, which may bias the scores. Technical: Claude wrote v0.2 and V-para items and the references, and judged. Self-preference direction is measured only by one post-hoc cross-family re-judge. v0.3 is to be written outside the Claude family.
- **bootstrap confidence interval** [G-bootstrap]: Resample the questions thousands of times to see how much the score could move by chance; the middle 95 % is the confidence interval. Technical: Stratified two-level bootstrap: items resampled within category, then runs within item; 2,000 resamples; paired for differences.
- **common-mode CI** [G-common-mode]: A wider interval that also counts run-to-run randomness, not just which questions were asked. Technical: CI_cm = point ± 1.96·sqrt(SE_item² + (σ_run/√seeds)²), σ_run raw 0.020 / floor-adjusted 0.025 on V-para (SEEDVAR). Used for deep-tier rows since 2026-10-11.
- **seed and seed noise** [G-seed-noise]: Running the same setup again with a different random seed gives different answers and a different score; that variation is seed noise. Technical: Seed = sampling seed of answer generation. Same deep configuration, s42 vs s43: Δ −0.039 (−0.072…−0.011) raw on V-para. Rule since 2026-10-16: a one-seed deep-tier Δ < 0.05 on V-para is noise (was 0.03).
- **judge noise (test-retest)** [G-judge-noise]: Re-judging the exact same answers changes the score by almost nothing. Technical: σ_judge ≈ 0.003 raw / 0.004 floor-adjusted, per-item r 0.96, winner 51/51 (Opus, 102 calls): an order of magnitude below seed noise.
- **MDE (minimum detectable effect)** [G-mde]: The smallest improvement a test of a given size can reliably tell apart from noise. "Not detected" below it does not mean "no effect". Technical: House MDEs: v0.2 one seed ≈ 0.025 raw (110 ids); V-para two seeds ≈ 0.03, one seed ≈ 0.06; 20-item slice ≈ 0.08; v0.1 EN 57 one seed ≈ 0.055.
- **paired comparison** [G-paired]: Two versions are compared on exactly the same questions, so question difficulty cancels out. Technical: Per-item differences with a paired bootstrap CI; the default reading for every arm (Δ = arm − named control).
- **winner's curse** [G-winners-curse]: If you pick the best of many setups on the same questions, it will look a bit better than it really is. Technical: The recommended deep configuration was the best of ≥ 8 deep configurations on V-para; expect a ≈ 0.01–0.02 discount on new data.
- **frozen criterion (pre-registration)** [G-frozen-criterion]: Before running an experiment, the pass/fail rule is written down and fingerprinted, so it cannot be bent to fit the result afterwards. Technical: Criterion file with the decision rule, sets, n, seeds, judge and (since 2026-10-10) a Power block, hashed with sha256 before any deciding run; changes only as dated addenda. 65/65 hashes verified on 2026-10-10; an outside critic matched 16 of 17 on 2026-10-16.
- **Power / Ceiling / Budget / Transfer blocks** [G-rule29]: Required parts of every experiment plan: how big an effect is expected, the best it could possibly do, what it may cost, and where it must also work. Technical: Power: expected Δ with source, n, seeds, MDE (2026-10-10). Ceiling: 0-call upper bound; launch only if ≥ 2×MDE. Budget: smoke, wall-clock caps, kill points. Transfer: a dev win must also show on the bounty-shaped set (Rule 29, 2026-10-14).
- **arm (experiment)** [G-arm]: One experiment: one change compared against a fixed control, with one frozen rule and one verdict. Technical: Directory arms/<arm>/ with criterion (+ sha256), patch, traces, aggregates and a verdict report. 159 verified arm records by 2026-10-13.
- **lever and verdict labels** [G-lever]: A "lever" is a change that measurably improves quality. Each experiment ends with a label saying whether it was one. Technical: LEVER = CI low > 0 and Δ ≥ +0.03; SMALL = real but below +0.03; NO = rule failed or CI excludes the hoped-for gain; "not detected at MDE x (n, seeds)" = CI straddles 0 below power; CHEAPER-PARITY = equal quality at lower cost.
- **step-0 gate (0-call gate)** [G-step0]: A free, automatic check an idea must pass before any expensive AI judging; most ideas died here. Technical: Deterministic gate with 0 judge calls (retrieval replay, coverage counts, byte-identity), e.g. "fact chunk in top-8 ≥ bar". Of 101 closed arms counted on 2026-10-13, 54 closed at 0 judge calls.
- **judge funnel (L0 → L1 → L2)** [G-funnel]: Cheap checks first, expensive judging last. Technical: L0 deterministic replay/unit gates → L1 Haiku on a random stratified 20-item slice → L2 Opus, two seeds, full set; held-out sets only by script, totals only.
- **random20 slice** [G-random20]: A fixed random sample of 20 questions used only to stop hopeless ideas early, never to measure an effect. Technical: Stratified random 20-id slices (seed 7) replaced the "20 hardest items" signal20 slice on 2026-10-10; futility screen only (one n = 20 Haiku pass carries ± 0.03–0.04 noise).
- **oracle / ceiling cell** [G-oracle]: A deliberately "cheating" run that hands the model the right fact, to measure the most any fix in that area could ever gain. Technical: Inject the item's known fact chunk as an extra kept passage: +0.095 (+0.070…+0.122) on the 180 pack self-tests (Opus) → headroom is retrieval reach, not reading.
- **closed registry (CLOSED.md)** [G-closed-registry]: A public list of every idea that did not work, with how strong the test was and what new evidence would justify trying again. Technical: One row per closed lever family: arm(s), set, n, seeds, judge, result, MDE, label and a "would reopen it" line. 76 public rows (R1–R76); 98 rows privately by 2026-10-16.
- **exposure ledger** [G-exposure-ledger]: A log with one line for every time a held-out question set was run or read, so readers can judge how clean each number is. Technical: 126 dated rows (55 v0.2-only, 33 V-para-only, 19 both); determines each set's status ruling (SET-STATUS).
- **held-out (blind) set** [G-held-out]: Exam questions that the people (and agents) building the app never see; the only fair test. Technical: Item text stored outside builder-readable paths; run only by script, aggregates only; every touch logged in the exposure ledger.
- **burnt set** [G-burnt]: A held-out set whose questions leaked to where builders could see them; from then on it only counts as practice data. Technical: v0.2: plaintext entered the private bench repository history; 12 generation runs and 10 Opus passes → burnt → dev by ruling 2026-10-06.
- **dev vs dev-style** [G-dev-style]: Dev = practice questions you may tune on. Dev-style = a set that was a fair exam once but has since been used to make decisions. Technical: V-para is held-out for the two 4B claim runs (2026-10-05/06) and dev-style for everything measured after 2026-10-06 (ruling 2026-10-09).
- **bench v0.1 / v0.2 / v0.3** [G-bench-versions]: Versions of the test set: a public practice set, a former exam set now used for practice, and a future exam to be written outside the Claude family. Technical: v0.1: 60 public items (57 EN judged), references public. v0.2: 110 items, burnt → dev. v0.3: items to be written by the author with a non-Claude model; hashes public before scoring; 0 items as of 2026-10-17.
- **V-para** [G-vpara]: A set of 58 questions (51 judged + 7 venue questions) written paraphrased from Vitalik Buterin's public blog posts; the set closest to the bar. Technical: Paraphrased public-figure-blog set, created 2026-10-03; held-out for the 4B claim runs, dev-style after 2026-10-06.
- **180 pack self-tests** [G-selftests]: Twenty public practice questions per knowledge pack, written from the pack's own text, used to see whether a pack helps. Technical: 9 packs × 20 agent-written items whose fact sits in a known chunk; lets the retrieval chain be traced end to end. Dev data, never claim evidence.
- **reference length share (LENGTHSHARE)** [G-lengthshare]: Some of the gap to the reference is just that the reference is longer. Technical: Re-judging the same answers against references condensed to ≤ 80 words: floor-adjusted 0.412 → 0.514, ≈ 17 % length / 83 % substance (1 seed, dev-style).
- **cut (frozen build)** [G-cut]: A numbered, locked version of the app's answering engine. Cut 7 is the version the claim is about. Technical: Cuts 1–7 bundle the arms that passed: cut 3 = cut 2 + verdict guard v3 + arithmetic tools + snakebite text; cut 4 adds KVFIX + instant-fragment guards; cut 5 adds a no-source KV prefix + route-gate skip; cut 6 adds DETMAX; cut 7 = cut 6 + FACTS r6 (frozen 2026-10-07).
- **fast tier (4B default)** [G-fast-tier]: The default answer path: a small 4-billion-parameter model reads a few retrieved passages and writes a short answer with sources. Technical: Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 Q4_K_M (≈ 2.5 GB, Apache-2.0) on cut 7; fits an 8 GB phone; p50 ≈ 31 s on a 12 GB-class phone.
- **deep tier** [G-deep-tier]: An optional slower mode that uses a much bigger model which "thinks" before answering; the largest quality gain measured so far, but as of 2026-10-18 it has not run on a phone (a pending run). Technical: Cut 6 + Qwen3.6-35B-A3B UD-Q2_K_XL (≈ 3B active per token) + thinking budget 512: V-para floor-adjusted 0.436 (2 seeds, dev-style, Opus), desktop p50 ≈ 79 s; projected ≈ 1.9–2.8 min on a 12 GB phone.
- **lite tier** [G-lite-tier]: A smaller model option for phones with 4–6 GB of RAM: faster, less accurate, behind the same safety rules. Technical: Qwen3-1.7B Q4_K_M + X2b; matches the plain 4B only on the 180 pack-matched dev items; −0.111 vs the 4B on the claim kit; thinking stays off.
- **mixture of experts (MoE)** [G-moe]: A big model split into many small "expert" blocks, of which only a few work on each word, so it knows a lot but runs cheaper than its size suggests. Technical: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B: 35B total, ≈ 3B active parameters per token. 25–35B MoEs form one closed-book plateau ≈ 0.60 raw on v0.1 vs 0.487 for the 4B.
- **thinking budget** [G-thinking-budget]: How many hidden "reasoning" words the model may write before answering. 512 was as good as 1024 at about half the time. Technical: Cap on reasoning tokens; budget curve on V-para floor-adjusted: 0 → 0.33, 256 → 0.39, 512 → 0.44, 1024 → 0.42; 3072 no gain (model stops ≈ 1.3k tokens).
- **expert streaming (bmoe, LES)** [G-expert-streaming]: Loading only the expert blocks a word needs from storage, instead of keeping the whole big model in memory. Technical: bmoe v0.28.0 (the on-device engine) is judged-equivalent to llama-server on identical contexts. An OpenBMB llama.cpp fork (LES) runs the 35B in ≈ 2.8 GiB RSS on a desktop CPU at 14–17 tok/s, judged-equivalent; phone flash speed unmeasured.
- **quantization** [G-quant]: Storing a model's numbers with fewer bits so it fits on small devices, at some cost in quality. Technical: Deep model UD-Q2_K_XL (2-bit); 3-bit gave +0.004 (not detected, ≥ +0.03 excluded). Ternary 2B/4B/9B models were closed on speed or quality.
- **instant tier (deterministic answers)** [G-instant-tier]: Answers produced by plain code, without the AI model: calculators, a facts table, emergency numbers, first-aid cards. Technical: Router paths that bypass the LLM: arithmetic, unit and travel-distance calculators, nearby places, FACTS table, first-aid cards, verbatim cited health excerpts.
- **FACTS table** [G-facts-table]: A small table of facts from Wikidata that answers simple questions instantly, with a citation. Technical: 761 KB, 4,233 Wikidata entities; r6: precision 90–97 %, false positives 0/100 blind, recall 60–70 % (FAIL vs the frozen 75 %, shipped by decision in cut 7); fires 0 times on the held-out sets.
- **emergency detector (DETMAX)** [G-detector]: A small text classifier that spots emergencies and health questions so the app answers them with reviewed text instead of model guesses. Technical: 106 MB learned classifier + rules; DETMAX (cut 6) takes the max of the detector score over the conversation window and the current turn. Weights not released (base-model licence unknown).
- **first-aid cards** [G-cards]: Short, fixed instructions for 32 conditions, with sources and an emergency-number line. AI-reviewed, not clinically reviewed. Technical: Reviewed card texts served when the detector fires; on the default tier the model never writes health advice. Every mention must carry "AI-reviewed, not clinically reviewed".
- **HEALTHGAP / NUMGUARD** [G-healthgap]: Safety rules: questions about doses, poisoning and similar count as emergencies, and only a deterministic calculator may override a fired emergency. Technical: HEALTHGAP r2 widens the emergency predicate; NUMGUARD r3 lets only the calculator answer a numeric question the detector flagged (the lexicon guard was rejected as unsafe by review).
- **verdict guard (self-contradiction guard)** [G-verdict-guard]: A check that catches answers which say "yes" and then argue "no". Technical: Deterministic repair of verdict/body contradictions; v3 shipped in cut 3 after a reviewer gate; v4 failed (1 WRONG of 2 fires).
- **route gate** [G-route-gate]: A one-word decision by the model: search the libraries, or just chat. Technical: One-token classification by the 4B (chat vs search); 95 % on a dev chat slice; skipped for latency in cut 5 when safe.
- **knowledge pack** [G-pack]: A downloadable offline library (one file) of articles on one topic that the app searches before answering. Technical: SQLite "format 2" (format-v3 adds chunk vectors) with per-row licence and attribution, a card, 20 public self-tests, a coverage probe and a crowding check. Licences: PD, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA only. 11 packs published on 2026-10-13.
- **retrieval funnel: reach → keep → read** [G-reach-keep-read]: Reach: is the right passage found? Keep: does it survive the filter into the prompt? Read: does the model use it correctly? Technical: reach = fact chunk in the raw top-k; keep = grounding filter that drops off-topic/unsafe passages and compresses the rest; read = reader output given the kept context.
- **crowding** [G-crowding]: Installing many libraries at once lets a big general one take the few slots the right small one needed. Technical: All packs installed vs the matching pack only: −0.019 (−0.035…−0.003) Opus on the 180 self-tests; packs above 0.40 of kept slots are user-selected only.
- **HEADPASS-X2b** [G-x2b]: The one retrieval change that worked: also feed the model the passages next to the best-matching one, plus a few found by meaning, within a fixed size limit. Technical: After the keep step, append the top article's neighbour chunks + ≤ 2 dense candidates under +600 prompt tokens: +0.066 (+0.043…+0.091) Opus on the 180 (dev); inert on the claim kit.
- **PSS / RSS, TTFT, prefill / decode** [G-pss]: Ways to measure an app's memory and speed: how much memory it really uses, and how long until the first word appears. Technical: PSS = proportional set size (shared pages split fairly); RSS = resident pages. TTFT = time to first token. Prefill = reading the prompt; decode = generating tokens.
- **desktop proxy and device parity** [G-desktop-proxy]: The exact phone engine also runs on a Mac for fast testing; real phones confirmed that the Mac gives the same judged quality. Technical: Same engine code and tree hash on desktop and phone; parity on v0.1: 12 GB-class phone 0.529 = desktop 0.529 (Haiku); 8 GB phone 0.561 vs 0.558/0.560 (Opus).
- **no-network APK** [G-no-network-apk]: A build of the app with the internet permission removed, so the phone itself blocks any network access. Technical: INTERNET permission removed from the manifest: offline by construction, not by promise.
- **confident-wrong** [G-confident-wrong]: An answer that is wrong but stated with confidence, the most harmful failure; tracked separately. Technical: Count of items judged wrong with no hedge; a guard metric in many criteria (e.g. "confident-wrong not up").
- **coordinator, research lanes and independent reviewers** [G-coordinator]: The research was run by AI agents: one coordinator ran the experiments, specialist agents proposed ideas in their own areas, and separate reviewer agents checked wording and numbers. Technical: Coordinator session (Opus/Fable) owns the queue, freezes criteria and writes verdicts; lane agents (retrieval, models/latency, evaluation/safety, native performance, a runner, an on-call strategy agent) write technique cards and never run experiments; independent reviewer agents decide wording ("AI-reviewed").
- **unattended research loop and ticks** [G-loop]: For four days an AI coordinator woke up on a timer, advanced one experiment, logged it and scheduled its next wake-up, without a human. Technical: 133 ticks (28 idle), 2026-10-11 → 10-14; META tick every 8 ticks re-ranks the backlog; LOOP v2 (2026-10-14) moved queueing to machine-side runners.
- **handoff** [G-handoff]: A file that lets a fresh AI session take over the coordinator role with no memory of the previous one. Technical: Dated HANDOFF-*.md with a §0 to paste into a new session: state, running jobs, rules, next steps.
- **claim text** [G-claim]: The text for the bounty that says, with numbers and caveats, what BOAR does and does not achieve; it follows the current standing and is posted at closure. Technical: Claim draft v4.2.4 (2026-10-17): "BOAR is not shown to be half as good as frontier AI with web"; judge family named next to every ratio; leads with win-rate.
- **submitted app vs research engine** [G-native-app]: The app actually submitted to the bounty is the app maintainer's own rewrite; the research numbers describe the engine it partly ports. Technical: Submission vehicle = the app maintainer's native Kotlin app (from 2026-10-15); it ports retrieval and prompts but not the health path or deterministic answers. All research numbers are for the TypeScript research engine (cut 7).
- **ruler (bench v0.4)** [G-ruler-v04]: A fixed set of 75 test questions, frozen on 2026-10-20 before any system answered them, meant to give the final score of the submitted app. Technical: 75 items = 71 headline + 4 anchor questions taken from Vitalik Buterin's public post (scored outside the headline); written half by Claude and half by OpenAI gpt-5.6-sol via the Codex CLI, cross-fact-checked; sha f9ad2f2f. Not written outside the Claude family (a stated deviation). First exposed to two engines on 2026-10-24.
- **ruler spec (RULER-SPEC v1)** [G-ruler-spec]: The written procedure for scoring the ruler: which references, judges, metrics and gates, frozen before scoring. Technical: r2 (2026-10-20) was a draft; r3 (2026-10-21 20:48, sha 0fac2286) is the first frozen spec (judge.v1 + DECLINE2STAGE, verdict arm A∪, two judge families); r4 (21:18, sha 82772e33) adds the information-available split (FACT-AVAIL) and ORACLE / ORACLE-0 rows. Spec files are public in the research record (github.com/r4topunk/boar-research, rnd/research/coordinator/RULER-SPEC-v1-*.md).
- **U-total** [G-u-total]: The ruler's headline number: how much of a reference answer's useful content the app delivers, over all 71 headline questions. Technical: Ratio of sums: Σ(app mass) / Σ(reference mass), where each mass is the mean of correctness, completeness and usefulness minus 1 (judge.v1, 1-5 scale), so the rubric floor maps to 0; detected non-answers are set to 0 by DECLINE2STAGE; health items weigh 0.5 (weight-1 printed beside). Verdict statistic = the lower of the two judge families per bootstrap draw; "meets the bar" needs CI low > 0.50. A different metric from the judge.v1 V-para / v0.2 ratios: never compare across.
- **DECLINE2STAGE** [G-decline2stage]: A first check that gives zero to answers that do not try to answer, before the usual judge scores the rest. Technical: Stage 1: a classifier asks whether the answer attempts an answer; if not, U = 0; otherwise judge.v1 scores it unchanged. Adopted as RULING R1 (2026-10-21) after the decline arm was invalid under judge.v1 (a bare refusal scored U 0.016-0.058); a coordinator override of a frozen FAIL, accepted with conditions by the spec reviewer. The decline offset D 0.058 is kept as a sensitivity row (U_net).
- **FACT-AVAIL (information-available split)** [G-fact-avail]: Labels saying, for each ruler question, whether the answer is actually written in the installed knowledge packs. Technical: Pre-registered per item: AVAILABLE (≥ 0.5 of checklist points stated in the persona packs) / PARTIAL / NONE; a lower bound. On the 71 headline items: 19 / 25 / 27 (strict 15 / 23 / 33). Labels are quarantined until the result card; counts only.
- **A∪ (verdict arm)** [G-a-union]: The app with every persona knowledge pack installed at once, used for the verdict so no question gets a hand-picked pack. Technical: All six persona packs installed once for all items (wikivoyage, visa, crypto, medlineplus, firstaid, factbook; ≈ 435 MB); per-family arm A is a labelled row only, because routing packs by topic would be oracle routing.
- **ORACLE / ORACLE-0** [G-oracle-arms]: The same model answering the same questions with and without the supporting passages handed to it. Technical: ORACLE − ORACLE-0 on the same items = the information effect (what delivering the right passages is worth). Defined in spec r4; not run as of 2026-10-24.
- **outcome labels (CWS, correct-any, WU, WW, HU)** [G-outcome-labels]: Each answer is put into one class: right with the right source shown, right from memory, wrong with no warning, wrong with a warning, or an honest "unknown". Technical: Shares of n items labelled by Claude Haiku 5.5-low under the UXMATRIX protocol: CWS = correct with the right source shown; CFM = correct from memory; correct-any = CWS + CFM; WC/WU = wrong with no warning; WW = wrong with the warning line; HU = honest unknown; RW = refused wrongly. Not a ratio to a reference: never compare with the judge.v1 ratio or with U-total.
- **delivery (kept / delivered fact)** [G-delivery]: Whether the passage that holds the answer actually reaches the small model's prompt after search and compression. Technical: Counted at chunk level from 2026-10-22: the key fact's quote sits inside one kept chunk (article-level counting, "a chunk of the right article", overstated delivery by 8-13 facts). Article-level and chunk-level counts are never pooled.
- **conditional thesis** [G-conditional-thesis]: The author's closing claim, in the form the tests support: the app is right about two times in three when the right passage reaches the model.
